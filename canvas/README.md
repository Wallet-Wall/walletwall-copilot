# WalletWall canvas

The implementation lives inside the plugin, because a Copilot plugin ships its canvas with it: `plugin/com.github.copilot/extensions/walletwall-evidence-explorer/`. The folder and the canvas id keep their historical name to avoid churn; the canvas is presented to users as **WalletWall**. This directory only holds the contract the implementation must keep.

## What it shows (one evidence bundle)

**The Wall** (the centre of the panel), drawn as a Coinstellation: every source, authority, observation, claim, WalletWall derivation and the vault readiness keystone of the bundle as nodes of one deterministic graph, with the claims' subjects (chain, contract, wallet, stablecoin) beside the claims they are about. Each claim node carries exactly the support state the MCP server returned. Beside it, for the piece in focus: its state and at most six facts (support, freshness, finality, authority, conflicts, the limitation count), with Provenance (the `trace_provenance` lineage), Details (subject and value as presented, authority class, corroboration, supersession, policy), Limitations (each code with a plain-language description) and **Sources** one step away. **Trace** emphasises the selected claim's provenance on the same graph. Below: the **Wallprint** band, the **Readiness assembly**, a searchable list of every piece, the criteria table and "does not establish" list, a legend, a reserved **Receipt** row and a text version of The Wall. On a phone the same content is composed as a focused instrument: a header with the state strip, The Wall as the stage in a tall layout, the detail as a bottom sheet (peek, half, full); nothing is dropped and nothing is computed on the page. Design rules: `docs/CANVAS_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md`.

**The WallPrint disclosure view.** Opened with `{ "wallprint": "<lineage>" }` instead of a bundle, the same panel shows one bundled synthetic WallPrint disclosure lineage as an editorial disclosure document: the subject as recorded, the current WalletWall assessment (the focal claim's recorded support state), why WalletWall says this (source authority, freshness, finality, independent corroboration, conflicts, limitations, invariant violations), the record's disclosure lifecycle ledger, every assessment as recorded, the provenance chain the record carries, corrections and supersessions with the engine's semantic diff, and a Verify section that asks four separate questions (Record: is this a structurally valid WallPrint record; Digest: do these canonical bytes produce the embedded digest; Receipt: does the disclosed record match the committed record; Replay: can the historical evaluation be reproduced under the recorded context), each answered only by the engine's recorded result, followed by a registration readout of every identifier, digest, version and instant and a derivation chain from the canonical payload to the commitment comparison. Every value is an output of the canonical WallPrint engine (`packages/mcp/src/wallprint`), serialized at generation time by `npm run gen:wallprint`; the page decides nothing. Principle: *WalletWall isn't always right. WallPrint keeps the record.*

## Contract

1. It renders structured results of the existing MCP tools; it does not compute states and does not add evidence. Every state comes from a call to the bundled `walletwall-mcp.mjs` made by the canvas itself, so the panel cannot drift from what the tools return. A state value outside the WWEP enumerations is displayed as "not recognised", never as a state. The Wall's layout is geometry only: it never ranks, scores or resolves evidence.
2. Same privacy posture: local only, no outbound network, no persistence, no telemetry. The one listener is loopback-only, which the Copilot app requires for canvases. Fonts (Sora, Manrope, IBM Plex Mono) and brand images (the WalletWall brick-wallet mark, WalletWallaby) are embedded as `data:` URIs; the page's CSP allows `data:` for fonts and images and nothing else beyond its own origin. See `docs/PLUGIN_PATH_PRIVACY.md`.
3. Read-only. No action signs, sends, swaps or writes.
4. No colour or wording that implies safety or approval: no green "verified" badge; `ESTABLISHED` is described as "presented evidence satisfies the published policy"; terracotta marks focus and attention (the piece in focus and its path, the Wallmark, conflict and staleness), never good or bad; every state is carried by ring, line, fill and label, not colour alone.
5. Agent-callable actions: `set_bundle`, `select_assertion`, `get_view`. `get_view` returns the MCP results the panel shows, so the agent describes the panel without re-deriving anything.
6. **Addresses** are printed in full in every detail, list and rail, exactly as the bundle presents them (no shortening, no case or checksum change), as selectable text, with the chain ID next to them and two controls: Copy (exactly the full value) and, when the chain is stated, Copy chain + address (`chainId:<id> address:<full value>`). A piece on The Wall shows a short label (Contract, Wallet) and never an address; its accessible name, tooltip and detail carry the full value. Nothing is stored because an address was copied, and the status line does not repeat the address.
7. The listener has four routes (`GET /`, `GET /view`, `GET /events`, `POST /select`), requires an unguessable path key and the exact `Host`, and refuses a foreign `Origin` or a non-JSON body on `POST /select`.
8. **Only the Observed lens exists.** Compare, Replay, Scenario, Receipt (Copy receipt, Verify) and QR are shown as disabled, reserved affordances with their reason on screen ("Requires immutable snapshot"; "Scenario — simulation engine not yet enabled"). Nothing in the evidence view generates a receipt, a QR code or a snapshot. The evidence view's Receipt row names its prerequisite in words: a receipt needs a committed WallPrint record of the evaluation (a record id, committed canonical bytes and a disclosure history), and a live bundle has only a bundle digest and an evaluation time, which identify no disclosure; the row links to nothing. The Receipt verification view and the portable reference with its QR code live in the WallPrint disclosure document (items 11 to 15).
9. The pieces settle once when the bundle changes and do not drift afterwards. Restrained loops (the conflict ring, the selection halo, stale recession, the traced path, the guide's idle) animate transform and opacity only, accompany states that are also drawn statically, and stop under reduced motion (system setting or the panel's own toggle). The WalletWallaby guide is optional and can be hidden; it is never needed to operate the panel.
10. **One selection, shared.** The selected assertion is the explorer's `selectedId`: a click on a claim sets it through `POST /select`, exactly as the agent's `select_assertion` does, and the page re-renders from the explorer's view. A click on any other piece is local visual focus only; it never becomes an assertion selection or a provenance request. The Wall is a projection of the explorer's view (`coinstellation-model.mjs`): it creates, strengthens or replaces no evidence state.
11. **The WallPrint view is a projection of serialized engine outputs.** `wallprint-fixtures.mjs` is generated from `packages/mcp/src/wallprint` by `scripts/gen-wallprint-fixtures.ts` (checked by `npm run verify:wallprint`): the record, its lifecycle events and lineage, the digest check, the validation findings, the receipt and its verification, the replay result and the semantic diff, all as the engine produced them. Two lineages are adversarial by construction (a record changed and re-digested before commit; a copy changed after commit without re-digesting), and one carries its issued receipt without the verification result (the view shows UNCHECKED and runs no check); each fixture declares its construction and the changed field, and the page prints it on the fixture line. The page computes no evidence state, lifecycle state, assessment, receipt or replay verdict, recomputes no digest and compares no assessor with another; a value outside the engine's vocabulary is shown as "not recognised", and absence is shown as absence (no result is not zero, unknown is not false, no assessment is not a failed assessment, a missing check is "not carried", never a failed check). Hierarchy: WallPrint record → serialized view input → view model → DOM, never the other way.
12. **Disagreement and history stay whole.** Every assessment is one row, as recorded; no consensus, overall verdict, badge or seal is shown, and the WalletWall row is never ranked above or below another. A correction or supersession keeps the earlier record visible and selectable with its own digest, lifecycle and receipt; a withdrawn record stays fully inspectable. Nothing is shown as deleted.
13. **Four verification questions stay separate, and receipt and replay keep their boundary.** The Verify section asks Record, Digest, Receipt and Replay as four checks and never combines them into one state. Each status word is a 1:1 projection of the engine's recorded result: a receipt is MATCH only for `matches: true` with no reason, MISMATCH only for `matches: false` with the engine's reasons (printed; told as "not compared" when the engine refused to compare, `RECEIPT_INVALID` or `RECORD_INVALID`), UNAVAILABLE when no receipt is carried and UNCHECKED when a receipt is carried without a verification result; anything else is "not recognised", never MATCH. Absence is neutral, never failure. A matching receipt is shown to establish that the disclosed canonical record matches its committed bytes and nothing more, with the engine's list of what it does not establish beside it. UNREPLAYABLE is printed as not INCORRECT: the missing or refused inputs are listed and never filled in. A mismatching receipt or a replay mismatch is shown with the engine's reasons, never as a judgement about the facts. Every version of a lineage keeps its own receipt: a record that is no longer current says so beside its receipt, and the other versions' receipts are listed as separate rows, never merged.
14. **Nothing new runs.** The view generates no receipt, snapshot or signature, reads no persisted record, imports nothing into the shipped MCP server, persists nothing in the browser and adds no route: it is served by the same four routes and the same page. The one thing it draws from a record is the portable reference's QR code (item 15), generated locally from the reference text. `select_assertion` is a no-op on a disclosure; `get_view` returns the recorded results (lifecycle, every assessment, digest check, receipt verification, replay, lineage, and the resolution of a reference it was opened from). A local jump (receipt, replay, history, assessments, provenance) focuses a server-rendered target inside the record in view; it is a button, not a link, and changes no URL or fragment.
15. **A portable reference is a locator, not authority.** Every disclosed record prints its portable reference (`spec/WALLPRINT.md`, "Portable reference"): `walletwall://wallprint/<record id>?reference=0.1&wallprint=0.1&digest=<committed digest>`, derived from the record id and the committed digest its `COMMITTED` lifecycle event recorded, never from a bundle digest; a record without a committed identity gets no reference and no code, and the prerequisite is named. The same text is drawn as a QR code by `wallprint-qr.mjs` (ISO/IEC 18004 byte mode, level M, versions 1 to 15, deterministic mask choice, a four-module quiet zone inside the drawing, the plate's ink on the paper, no library, no remote image service, no canvas element) beside the exact text with a Copy control, the record id, the committed digest, what the code encodes, the boundary sentence and, folded, the same reference pointing at each section and its JSON envelope. The code encodes the reference and nothing else: no wallet address, no web or local address, no session, no key, no query, no record content. It identifies which record and which bytes; it establishes nothing about the record's claims, carries no signature and is not a seal. Opened with `{ "reference": "<text or envelope>" }`, the panel parses the reference strictly with the canonical codec (`wallprint-reference.mjs`, generated from `packages/mcp/src/wallprint/reference.ts` by `npm run gen:wallprint-reference`), looks the record up by id across the bundled lineages (a corrected, superseded or withdrawn version is as valid a target as the current one and is shown as that version, never replaced by the current one), compares the reference's digest with the digest the record carries and the digest the engine recorded for it, and tells one of six outcomes: `RESOLVED` (the record is shown with the resolution above it and the referenced section focused), `DIGEST_MISMATCH` (the record is shown, every digest is printed, the section is not opened, and nothing is called forged), `NOT_COMPARED` (the record is shown, but the disclosure carries no digest or no engine-computed digest for it, so nothing was compared and nothing is said to match or differ: the plate's quiet edge, not an alarm), `UNAVAILABLE` ("Record unavailable": the store does not hold the record, or more than one record carries the id; not invalid; the id and digest are printed back as written and are not repeated in the agent summary), `MALFORMED_REFERENCE` and `UNSUPPORTED_VERSION` (a fixed problem code; no part of the text is echoed). A reference's id may not be address-shaped, credential-shaped or carry a reserved assurance term, so a printed-back id never reads as an address, a secret or a verdict. The page accepts no typed input, so opening a reference is the agent's `set_bundle`; the evidence view's QR control stays disabled, because a live bundle has no committed record identity. The canvas's code encodes the `walletwall:` reference itself, not a web address: no app registers a handler for it, so scanning it opens nothing by itself; opening it needs a compatible WalletWall disclosure store or resolver, such as this panel's bundled lineages or WalletWall's public resolver for published disclosures (item 16). The plugin names no endpoint, so the canvas never draws the HTTPS form.
16. **Public mode is the same instrument.** WalletWall's public resolver (`packages/wallprint-web`, `spec/WALLPRINT.md` "Public resolver", `docs/WALLPRINT_PUBLIC_RESOLVER.md`) renders a deliberately published disclosure with this same `wallprint-render.mjs`, `wallprint-styles.mjs`, `wallprint-client.mjs` and `labels.mjs`, given a `publication` block the canvas never passes. Public mode prints the publication line (record, digest, instant, how a synthetic lineage was constructed) instead of the fixture line, the boundary statement beside the principle, the record's standing as the store's index records it, lineage versions as same-origin links built only through the reference codec from a predecessor's or successor's committed digest (nothing redirects), and the HTTPS form of the reference in the QR code when a resolver base validates, with the `walletwall:` reference printed beside it. Every other outcome is an outcome page that renders no record content: `UNAVAILABLE`, `UNREACHABLE`, `NOT_COMPARED`, `DIGEST_MISMATCH` (the published version offered only as an explicit link; an artifact that fails its own check is flagged and shows nothing), `MALFORMED_REFERENCE`, `UNSUPPORTED_VERSION`. The page proves bytes (canonical form and SHA-256), checks the carried results and the standing's hash-chained ledger against the proved record before it renders, and says on every resolved page that the URL's digest covers the record and not the results carried beside it; it decides no state, no authority, no verdict. A copy served from another origin than the resolver base says so without naming that origin; the bare route is a landing sentence.

## Opening it

`open_canvas` with `canvasId: "walletwall-evidence-explorer"` and input `{ "fixture": "usdc-established" | "stale-identity" | "conflicting-balances" | "partial-provider-balance" | "unknown-balance" | "superseded-balance" | "readiness-blocked" }` for a bundled synthetic example, or `{ "bundle": <WWEP bundle>, "evaluatedAt": "<UTC time>" }` for the same input you pass to `inspect_evidence`, or `{ "wallprint": "current-supported" | "conflicting-assessors" | "corrected" | "superseded" | "withdrawn" | "unreplayable" | "receipt-valid" | "receipt-mismatch" | "replay-mismatch" | "conflicting-evidence" | "receipt-unreplayable" | "receipt-unverified" }` for a bundled synthetic WallPrint disclosure lineage, or `{ "reference": "walletwall://wallprint/<record id>?reference=0.1&wallprint=0.1&digest=<digest>[&target=<section>]" }` (or the JSON envelope) for a portable WallPrint reference, resolved against the bundled lineages (item 15).

## Deferred

Everything beyond one bundle and the Observed lens on The Wall: immutable snapshots of a live bundle, receipts or replay for anything but the bundled disclosure lineages, signing or attestation of references and records, a canvas control for external assessor import (the engine has a local, pure import boundary, `packages/mcp/src/wallprint/assessor-import.ts`; the canvas has no paste, upload or import control and computes none of its semantics), scenarios or simulation, multi-bundle comparison, export, editing evidence. The evidence view's Receipt row stays reserved and disabled until a committed WallPrint record of the live evaluation exists (item 8); the portable reference and its QR code exist inside the WallPrint disclosure document (item 15) and on the public resolver page for published records (item 16); a canvas control for external assessor import and any signing research are later lanes. See `docs/DESKTOP_INSTALL_PROOF.md` for what has and has not been verified inside the Desktop app.
